  ---
  What the real numbers say

  The uncomfortable finding

  Both DPO models over-refuse more than the base model. SafeComp is actually the
   worst (32.7%), not the best. This is the opposite of what the training was
  supposed to do for benign_sensitive prompts.

  Breaking it down:
  - 50% of prompts — all 3 models refuse (these may be genuinely
  hard/borderline)
  - 30% of prompts — all 3 models comply (easy benign questions)
  - 12% (142 prompts) — DPO-induced over-refusals: base helps, both trained
  models refuse
  - ~2% — SafeComp uniquely refuses but base and baseline comply

  Why is this happening?

  DPO training on benign_sensitive used chosen=helpful_answer, 
  rejected=hard_refusal — so it should reduce over-refusal. But the FalseReject
  dataset contains prompts that genuinely look borderline (machine safeguard
  bypasses, security screening liquids, redlining history) — and DPO likely
  over-fitted the safety signal from unsafe and dual_use categories, which are
  the majority of the training data, bleeding into benign_sensitive territory.

  What to report vs. what to be cautious about

  Report honestly:
  - WildGuard is not a valid scorer for over-refusal benchmarks — discard those
  numbers entirely
  - GPT-4o scores show DPO training increases refusal rates on benign_sensitive
  prompts for both models
  - Base Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is already quite refuse-heavy at 46.9% on this
  benchmark

  Be cautious about:
  - SafeComp being worse than baseline here (32.7 vs 36.6%) is notable — the
  extra dual_use safe_completion training doesn't transfer to benign_sensitive,
  and may even slightly hurt
  - This is a real limitation of v1 that's worth acknowledging in the paper, not
   hiding
  - The FalseReject dataset itself may have labeling noise (some "benign"
  prompts are genuinely borderline)
